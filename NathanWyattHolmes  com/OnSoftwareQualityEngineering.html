Software Quality Assurance

Process and expectations

  • Completely Testing a Software Product is Impracticable

    • Detailed analysis (code paths and exponential degrees of complexity, Environment, data boundaries, Etc.) of any software, more complex than a calculator, will yield a impractically large number of test cases that would need to be run.
      Because this is not easily understood by stakeholders who have not had experience in software QA it is important to communicate this clearly. And hopefully you will not be handed an impossible mandate like "0 bug count" from 2 management levels up, as this will only encourage the project wide whitewashing of QA reporting.
    • Priority in test planning as well as test sets and cycles are vital

  • QA Are Customer Advocates

Measuring Quality

  • Statistical analysis yields comprehensive cursory data while inversely its data for the most critical details is frequently inaccurate, deceptive, and falsely inverted.

    • It's common for management to rely on statistical analysis to gauge the quality level of a product. Statistical analysis of a software development project is normally a required, and extremely useful component software development. Even on projects with >3 Software Engineers and >2 QA Engineers it almost unexceptionally will show great utility.
      • Given the important role statistical analysis plays in the development lifecycle it's quite surprising how little consideration they are shown.
        • It's quite common for all levels of project management to completely ignore the specifics of how the reported data was calculated.
          Further, the data is often misinterpreted from ignorance regarding proper statistical analysis and theory.
          Common logical errors arise such as inferring causation through correlation or implementing a process correction based on variations falling within the standard deviation.
          • Inaccuracies are acutely compounded as proper safeguards against competing interests are not put in place. Any incentive towards a particular result within the group of people having stewardship of data used will foster an unacceptable amount of risk of deliberate falsification of data. This alone can easily damage a project to the point of gross failure!
        Unfortunately, professionally I've most often observed organizations mis-manage the process of gathering, reporting, and interpretation of statistics to the point where the project was experiencing significant setbacks as a result. This became so severe in one case that the product delivery was over two times it's budget and three times it's timetable. The estimates on V1 delivery and overall quality had become so inaccurate that eventually a hard decision at the executive level and the hammer was brought down despite millions in losses. The entire division of around 200 employees were laid off over the course of less than 2 months. The higher up the management chain the greater the danger of losing context on the reliability of the numbers.

    • You cannot measure something without changing it.

      Placing value on a statistic as part of the QA process increases the risk that statistic will become skewed.
      For example, using a products bug count as a measure of how close the product is to release.

    • QA should be insulated from the drive for better quality numbers (not better quality)

      The QA department should measure its performance using statistics that are outside the statistics used as quality level indicators. Performance evaluation for QA should be based on its delivery of accurate quality reporting, not the quality levels being reported.
